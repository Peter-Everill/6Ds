# 6Ds methodology — overview

The **6Ds** provides a business-led progression from understanding economic performance to identifying, implementing and measuring improvements.

| D | Focus | Illustrative output |
|---|---|---|
| Drivers | Economic model and value drivers | Economic driver tree and value pools |
| Defect | Material performance gaps | Defined performance problem and potential value at stake |
| Diagnosis | Competing explanations | Evidence-based hypotheses and validation needs |
| Drilldown | Decision and process mechanisms | Decision/process definition, causal pathways and measurement grain |
| Doable | Feasible interventions | Prioritised improvement cases and tests |
| Dashboard | Monitoring and value realisation | Execution, outcome, economic and guardrail measures |

**Interpretation note:** This is a concise public explanation of the named 6Ds stages. The five uploaded Custom GPT specifications detail a *related five-stage analytical implementation*, not a canonical six-document definition of the methodology. The stages overlap and iterate; they should not be treated as a rigid one-to-one correspondence.

## Decision Loss and Process Loss

- **Decision Loss:** Avoidable economic value lost because a choice was inferior to a better feasible choice.
- **Process Loss:** Avoidable economic value lost because work was designed, coordinated or executed worse than a feasible alternative, including failure to turn a sound choice into value.
- **Unattributed avoidable loss:** Potentially avoidable value not yet reliably assigned to either mechanism.

A bad outcome does not itself prove avoidable loss. Establish the relevant feasible comparator, account for uncertainty and constraints, and avoid overlapping attribution.

## Applying the method

Start with the business economics; define the problem, scope and decision or activity; map the causal pathway; collect evidence at the relevant event grain; quantify avoidable loss where supported; compare interventions; and test economic improvement.

**AI is an option, not the objective.** The preferred intervention is the least complex feasible change that reliably recovers value.
