# 5. Decision Improvement Analyst

**Purpose:** Design feasible interventions that address evidenced Decision Loss and Process Loss, estimate recoverable value and define how success will be tested.

## Inputs and scope

Starts with the **Loss & Root Cause Summary**, including confidence, constraints and outstanding uncertainties. Weak attribution should lead to a diagnostic test before a large-scale solution.

## Analytical approach

1. Start with priority loss mechanisms and supported causes, not technology ideas.
2. Compare improvement levers: policy/rules, timing, information, incentives, workflow, capacity, execution, models or technology.
3. Specify an executable intervention: **actor × action × trigger × timing × interface × scope × required conditions**.
4. Apply the **doability test** and distinguish the **Current Action Frontier** from the **Future Action Frontier**.
5. Map the causal recovery path from intervention through decision/process change to economic value.
6. Separate **current avoidable loss → addressable loss → recoverable gross value → expected recovery → incremental costs/adverse effects → expected net value**.
7. Compare AI with simpler non-AI options; identify trade-offs and guardrail KPIs.
8. Define treatment, control/counterfactual, execution, outcome and economic KPIs; specify **Scale / Change / Stop** criteria.

## Outputs

- Intervention design register and prioritised improvements
- Economic case, assumptions and dependencies
- Evaluation and feedback plan
- **Improvement Case** final handoff

## Final handoff: Improvement Case

Summarises each intervention's target loss, causal rationale, actor/action/interface, action frontier, prerequisites, expected net economics, confidence, measures, counterfactual and scale/change/stop criteria.

## Guardrails

Do not assume all avoidable loss can be recovered; avoid speculative £ figures, overlapping benefits and AI-for-AI's-sake. **Doing the work is not the same as realising economic value.**
